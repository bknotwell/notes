#+title: F and t test example
#+OPTIONS: ^:nil author:nil html-postamble:nil num:nil
#+SETUPFILE: ./latexcss.theme
#+HTML_HEAD_EXTRA: <style> pre.src { background-color: black; color: white; }</style>

This document shows how to compare means and variances in a rigorous
way using a [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student%27s_t-test][t-test]] or an [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-test][F-test]] to determine the statistical
significance of the differences. The following example uses four
vector containing 250 elements of [[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_distribution][normally distributed]] data with means
in the range $[115 - 124]$ and deviations in the range $[8 - 15]$.

#+begin_src R :session R-tests :exports both :results output graphics file :file tests-boxplot.svg
set.seed(33333333)
one <- rnorm(250, 120, 10)
two <- rnorm(250, 124, 10)
three <- rnorm(250, 120, 8)
four <- rnorm(250, 115, 15)
boxplot(one, two, three, four)
#+end_src

** 1 vs 2 mean and variance

We expect 1 and 2 to have similar variances and different means.  Visually, it looks like 2 is more variant.

#+begin_src R :session R-tests :exports both :results output
print(t.test(one, two))
print(var.test(one, two))
#+end_src

The t-test is definitive (2.485e-05 is a tiny number) and we can be confident the means are different.  The variance's F-test shows the ratios of the variances to be different but we can't be confident they are as we expect this ~80% variance ratio to occur randomly about 13.3% of the time.  If we had relied on the graphic to flag a change in variance, it would've been a false positive.

** 1 vs 3 mean and variance

We expect one and three to have identical means and different variance:

#+begin_src R :session R-tests :exports both :results output
print(t.test(one, three))
print(var.test(one, three))
#+end_src

The means show minimal differences that we'd expect to show up randomly 34% of the time.  The ratio of variances is close to what we expect but we still shouldn't be confident there's a difference as we'd expect this result randomly 18.7% of the time.

** 1 vs 4 mean and variance

We expect the mean and variance to show up as different:

#+begin_src R :session R-tests :exports both :results output
print(t.test(one, four))
print(var.test(one, four))
#+end_src

We can be confident both statistics differ between the vectors as this result's unlikely to have occurred by chance.
